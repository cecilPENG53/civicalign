Smarter decisions for a growing city

See what Council's plan for Woodhouse Square means, then have your say.

People who live, work and shop around CBD South Parramatta can't realistically read the long reports behind the draft master plan, so they can't give informed input on how the new 1,350 m² Woodhouse Square S2 p.23 should be shared. CivicAlign turns those reports into a visual, source-linked storyboard, invites residents to take part, and sums up their feedback for Council's planners to use in decisions and in the next round of planning.

01 · The idea

Council has done the professional work. Residents can't read it.

The draft master plan S2, the Council report S4 and the Smart City Strategy S5 each run to dozens of pages. The brief asks entries to build on that work, interpreting it and filling evidence gaps rather than duplicating it S1 p.13. CivicAlign does exactly that, for one decision in one place.

ALREADY DECIDED BY COUNCIL
  • A dining and nightlife precinct with a civic square, chosen over a large park and a small convention centre S4
  • The plaza goes on Wentworth Street, on Council-owned land S2 p.23, after scoring options against principles P1–P6 S2 p.22
LEFT OPEN FOR RESIDENTS

How to share the square. The plan lists shade trees, public art, lighting, flexible paving for night markets and events, outdoor dining and raised planters with seating S2 p.44–45, but not how much room each gets.

1 · WHAT THE PLAN SAYS

Read the plan in plain words

The three things Council promises residents (the square, the Chinatown nightlife destination and the traffic changes S3), with what was ruled out and what is still open. Every claim opens to its source page.

2 · SEE IT, THEN TRY ANOTHER WAY

A square you can rearrange

A 2.5D picture of the square, starting from the plan as drawn. Move the sliders and trees, market stalls, benches and paths change in place. A side view shows what each change affects, as labelled ranges rather than forecasts.

3 · TAKE PART

Say what you've seen, see who acts

Tick what you have run into next to what Council says, pick between uses in quick rounds, and add your own experience, with no typing needed. Each use shows whether Council delivers it, partners on it or must advocate for it S5 p.33.

THE FEEDBACK LOOP

Residents understand the plan → take part → Council sees their views summarised: by option and by role, only for groups of 10 or more, with a representativeness check and each item tagged Deliver, Partner or Advocate → planners use it to weigh decisions, close evidence gaps and prepare the next round, such as the Phase 2 exhibition in 2027 S3.

How we will know it works

3-minute test
A tester can name the trade-off, the evidence, the uncertainty and who is responsible within 3 minutes S1 p.12.
Groups of 10+
How many resident groups reach the 10-person reporting threshold, and how they compare with the ABS Census.
"Baseline TBC"
Resident input can help set strategy indicators whose baseline is still "TBC", such as satisfaction with climate adaptation and wayfinding S5 p.39, p.63.
02 · Testing

What testing taught us

We asked testers to use the prototype, then say what the trade-off is, what evidence supports it, how uncertain it is and who is responsible. That is the brief's 3-minute test S1 p.12. Two lessons changed the design.

LESSON 1 · WHAT TESTERS TOLD US

Show the plan by what matters to people, not by the PDF.

Testers wanted to judge the plan by dimensions such as the economy, education, multicultural life and inclusion, so they could see whether it is worth it. Long PDFs and government wording lost them.

Our summary of testers' comments, translated from Chinese.

WHAT WE CHANGED
  • Dimensions, not documents. The plan is shown across 9 dimensions, and each person picks the three they care about most.
  • Economy appears as economic activity, multicultural life as culture and social life plus 13 languages, and inclusion as a dimension of its own.
  • Plain words first. Every Council claim is rewritten in plain words, with the original one tap away.
  • Education is not covered yet. It is the first dimension on our next-step list, and it goes in only with a source behind it.
LESSON 2 · WHAT OUR SATURDAY TEST ROUND SHOWED

Prediction was the wrong centre of gravity.

Our interaction leaned towards technical AI model analysis and prediction. That did not solve the real pain point: residents need to see and understand what Council has proposed, and to take part.

WHAT WE CHANGED
  • The plan comes first. The visual explanation and resident participation lead. The model is a supporting view, labelled as assumptions.
  • Feedback closes the loop. Resident views are summarised for Council staff to analyse, decide and judge, and to inform the next round of planning.

Evidence gaps we found

  • No traffic model yet: consultant work comes after Phase 2, planned for FY27/28 S2 p.54 S4
  • Funding for the square is not settled S2 p.54
  • Three sizes: 1,200 m² S2 p.23, 1,300 m² S3 or at least 1,350 m² S2 p.51. We use 1,200 m² and flag it.
  • Whether trees get deep soil: the car park is partly underground, but no source says whether its basement extends under the square S2 p.45, p.49

Where no source gives a figure, the prototype says so rather than filling it in.

Swapping simulated votes for real ones

Collect responses on Council's own Participate Parramatta platform, which has had more than 196,000 unique visitors since 2020 S5 p.16–17, plus QR signs on site and library tablets. Ask only the minimum S5 p.30 under Council's existing privacy notice, then check representativeness against the ABS Census. That is lawful and low-cost because it reuses channels Council already runs.

05 · Privacy

No sensitive or unnecessary personal information

Minimum data only

The prototype asks for a role (local resident, business or worker, commuter, visitor), an optional age band and an optional postcode, following the strategy's minimum-data principle S5 p.30. There are no names, emails, addresses or logins.

Nothing leaves the browser

The prototype has no server. Votes and comments are saved only in the visitor's own browser and are not sent anywhere. The page loads web fonts from Google Fonts.

Groups of 10 or more

Council sees results only for groups of at least 10 people, so no small group can be singled out. The demo shows this: its simulated visitor group has 8 people and stays hidden.

06 · Responsible AI

AI explains and extracts. People check. AI produces no numbers.

The brief asks for a human accountability point wherever AI is used S1 p.3, p.13.

AI inside the product

  • AI extracts claims from Council PDFs. A claim without a source, page and quote is dropped.
  • A planner approves, edits or rejects each claim. Only approved claims reach residents.
  • AI drafts advocacy briefs. A person approves them.
  • AI produces no numbers, makes no decisions and publishes nothing on its own.

Known risks

  • The impact model is not yet validated. Its ranges rest on team assumptions and need more calibration, testing and cross-validation before anyone relies on them.
  • Simulated votes could be read as real opinion, so every count is labelled.
  • AI translations may shift meaning until native speakers have reviewed them.
  • People who use a web tool are not everyone, so we add offline channels and an ABS check.
07 · Council's role and fit with Council's plans

Council delivers it

Council would run CivicAlign on Participate Parramatta. Its planners approve every claim residents see and receive the results. Inside the square, each use carries its own role, using the strategy's Deliver, Partner and Advocate labels S5 p.33. The split below is our proposal.

D+PCouncil leads with partnersEvents and night markets, bikes, recycling
PMainly partnersCultural expression: artists, Chinese community groups, First Nations groups
ACouncil advocatesMatters for others to decide, such as using data to press the NSW Government on transport S5 p.44

Which strategy it supports

The Smart City & Innovation Strategy 2024–2033 and its six priorities S5 p.8–9. CivicAlign maps to these actions:

1.2.22.1.12.1.22.1.42.2.23.1.23.1.34.1.24.1.34.2.15.1.36.1.16.2.26.3.2

For example: 2.1.1, better local heat data S5 p.38; 6.1.1, closing digital access gaps S5 p.62; 6.2.2, wayfinding S5 p.62.

How it differs from what Council already does

The plan's public exhibition (11 May to 23 June 2026) published the documents and collected submissions S3. CivicAlign does not redo the plan. It explains the plan claim by claim, lets residents test other splits against the plan as drawn, and returns structured, threshold-checked feedback plus a list of evidence gaps. That is what the brief asks for S1 p.13.

08 · Our next step

A service design test in a real consultation

Phase 2 of the master plan goes on exhibition in 2027 S3. We propose testing CivicAlign with Council's CBD South team before then.

  1. Run it for 4–6 weeks (our estimate) on Participate Parramatta, with QR signs at the square site and tablets in the library.
  2. Measure it with the 3-minute test S1 p.12, the number of groups reaching 10 people, and a representativeness check against the ABS Census.
  3. Replace simulated data with real data: real responses replace the 96 simulated respondents, collected through Council's own channels with minimum data S5 p.30.
  4. Validate the model: calibrate the coefficients against literature and Council's technical studies as they arrive, such as the traffic study S2 p.54, then test and cross-validate.
  5. Add the dimensions testers asked for, starting with education, each with a cited source before it appears.
  6. Review the translations with native speakers, starting with Chinese: people of Chinese background are the largest cultural group in the Parramatta LGA, at 22.3% S3.
